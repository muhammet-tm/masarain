Two paths, one call

Masarain forecasts the next 1.5 seconds of the active network from signals the device already has. While the risk lasts, each voice packet travels on two networks, inside a data budget.

Masarain: the same seconds

A break in the tape is a gap the listener hears, and the white strip marks the seconds when a second copy was sent. The tapes are one 60-second run of the prototype over emulated links, recorded on 8 October 2026. We chose this stretch of the campus walk as an illustration, because it shows the fade, the copies, the move, and the drop in order; the averages are in the results below. In this run, switch after failure lost 9.9% of the voice with a longest silence of 1.5 s. Masarain lost 0.4% and sent 17% extra data as copies. It also carried the call itself on 5G for 41 of the 60 seconds, which the extra-data figure does not count.

A moving user loses the call at every network boundary

Wi-Fi, 5G, satellite, and wired links fail in different ways when a user moves: coverage fades, cells hand over, Starlink satellite links reconfigure every 15 seconds, and cables are unplugged.

A device normally keeps one network active and changes only after that network has failed. In our simulation this loses 5.3% of the voice packets and leaves 14.7% of the seconds with degraded audio.

A second copy travels only while a forecast or a detected loss flags the link

Masarain is a session layer on the device. It estimates the risk that the active network will lose packets within the next 1.5 seconds, and it steps up an escalation ladder only as far as that risk requires.

  1. Normal

    One copy of each packet travels on the preferred path.

    No extra data

  2. Risk ahead

    When the forecast loss probability reaches 5%, or a loss has just been detected, a second copy of each critical packet travels on the backup path. Each copy costs one token.

    Budget: 20% of the critical traffic in the long run. The bucket starts full, so a short run can exceed 20%. The risk required for a copy rises as the budget drains.

  3. Risk sustained

    When the risk stays at 15% or more for 1 second, the call moves to the safer path. It returns after 3 seconds of healthy signal.

    Copies resume only if the new path is at risk

One risk estimate from the signals each network offers

Wi-Fi

The trend of the received signal strength over the last 2 seconds is projected 1.5 seconds ahead and converted to a loss probability.

5G

The trend of the serving cell's signal strength, which falls before a handover or a coverage hole.

Satellite

The reconfiguration clock of Starlink, known from published measurements: a brief disturbance every 15 seconds at known seconds of the minute.

Wired

No warning exists for a pulled cable, so the link-down signal triggers the ladder at once.

Why a budget

A token bucket limits copies to a fixed long-run share of the critical traffic. It keeps the cost of copies on a metered backup link predictable, and it bounds the copies sent when a forecast is wrong or when someone feeds the device false signals.

Paired simulation first, then the real prototype over emulated links

The radio links are emulated. No result on this page comes from a real radio network.

  1. Step 1

    Simulator

    A packet-level simulator written for this project replays six mobility scenarios on a 10 ms link grid. Wi-Fi, 5G, satellite, and wired links each have their own failure model, and the forecaster never reads the true link state.

  2. Step 2

    Comparison

    Eight mechanisms meet exactly the same losses and delays in each run, over 30 random seeds per scenario. We report differences as paired means with 95% intervals, and we compare the budgeted families at equal budgets.

  3. Step 3

    Prototype

    The prototype is real software: a client and a server with an encrypted packet format, real timers, and the same policy module as the simulator. A relay between them replays the simulator's link traces.

Limits of this work

  • The link models are synthetic. Many parameters are assumptions, and the sensitivity runs vary the main ones.
  • Most of the gain comes from the budgeted ladder. A fixed signal threshold inside the same ladder already reaches 0.81% loss, and the forecast improves this to 0.65%.
  • Sudden failures cannot be forecast. The forecaster flags 0% of pulled cables and 14% of satellite obstructions in advance, and there Masarain behaves like reactive duplication.
  • Early warning has a price: 54% of Wi-Fi alarms and 44% of 5G alarms are not followed by real degradation. The budget keeps those false alarms affordable.
  • The prototype has run over emulated links only. A field test on real Wi-Fi and 5G is the next step.
