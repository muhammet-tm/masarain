Two paths, one call

Masarain forecasts the next 1.5 seconds of the active network from signals a device can observe. While the risk lasts, each packet with a deadline, from a call, a video call, or a control link, travels on two networks inside a data budget. Buffered video and downloads move to the safer network when the risk persists, but they are never copied.

The tapes are one 60-second run of the prototype over emulated links, recorded on 8 October 2026 on the link models of that day; it was not repeated after the models were set from published measurements. We chose this stretch of the campus walk as an illustration, because it shows the fade, the copies, the move, and the drop in order; the averages are in the results below. In this run, switch after failure lost 9.9% of the voice with a longest silence of 1.5 s. Masarain lost 0.4% and sent 17% extra data as copies. It also carried the call itself on 5G for 41 of the 60 seconds, which the extra-data figure does not count.

Calls, video calls, and control links break when the active network drops

Wi-Fi, 5G, satellite, and wired links fail in different ways when a user moves: coverage fades, cells hand over, Starlink satellite links reconfigure every 15 seconds, and cables are unplugged.

Real-time traffic has a deadline. A voice packet that arrives more than 80 ms after it was sent is useless, and so is a video-call packet after 150 ms or a control message after 250 ms. A device normally keeps one network active and changes only after that network has failed. In our simulation this loses 5.5% of the voice packets and leaves 14.4% of the seconds with degraded audio.

A second copy travels only while a forecast or a detected loss flags the link

Masarain is a session layer on the device. It estimates the risk that the active network will lose packets within the next 1.5 seconds, steps up an escalation ladder only as far as that risk requires, and copies only the packets that lose their value after a deadline.

  1. Normal

    One copy of each packet travels on the preferred path.

    No copies; only keepalive probes on the idle path

  2. Risk ahead

    When the forecast loss probability reaches 5%, or a loss has just been detected, a second copy of each packet with a deadline travels on the backup path. Each copy costs one token. Buffered video and bulk data stay on one path.

    Budget: 20% of the traffic with a deadline in the long run. The bucket starts full, so a short run can exceed 20%. The risk required for a copy rises as the budget drains.

  3. Risk sustained

    When the risk stays at 15% or more for 1 second, every flow moves to the safer path, buffered video and bulk data included. The flows return after 3 seconds of healthy signal.

    Copies resume only if the new path is at risk

One risk estimate from the signals each network offers

Wi-Fi

The trend of the received signal strength over the last 2 seconds is projected 1.5 seconds ahead and converted to a loss probability.

5G

The trend of the serving cell's signal strength, which falls before a handover or a coverage hole.

Satellite

The reconfiguration clock of Starlink, known from published measurements: a brief disturbance every 15 seconds at known seconds of the minute.

Wired

No warning exists for a pulled cable. The path simply stops answering, and the flows move once no acknowledgment has arrived for four smoothed round-trip times, and for at least 100 ms. That is well before the network card reports the cable as unplugged after 0.75 s.

Why a budget

A token bucket limits copies to a fixed long-run share of the traffic with a deadline. It keeps the cost of copies on a metered backup link predictable, and it bounds the copies sent when a forecast is wrong or when someone feeds the device false signals.

Masarain is a session layer over UDP that knows what each flow carries

It works between the applications and the transport layer, so the network, the operator, and the applications stay unchanged. Other multipath mechanisms work lower in the stack.

Where each multipath mechanism works

Multipath TCP
Transport layer, in the kernel: one TCP connection spread over several paths.
Multipath QUIC
Transport layer in user space, over UDP; when to send a copy is left open.
3GPP ATSSS
In the 5G device and the core network: the operator steers or splits traffic between 5G and Wi-Fi.
SD-WAN
A network-layer overlay on an edge router that steers or duplicates traffic by policy.
Masarain
A session-layer overlay over UDP, on the device or on a gateway; no operator is needed.

How a flow gets its class

In a deployment, the application names the class when it opens a flow; on a gateway, the DSCP mark, the protocol, and the ports of each packet give it. Masarain reads headers only, and the payload stays encrypted end to end. This is the design: in the simulator, each class runs alone on the same links, and the prototype carries only a voice call and a control stream, so it does not classify flows yet.

Each network fails in its own way, and each model follows published measurements

The simulator gives each access type its own failure model. Its parameters come from measurement studies of campus Wi-Fi, of commercial 5G while driving, and of Starlink at fixed sites and in motion, and from IEEE, 3GPP, and ITU-R documents and the documentation of operating systems. Each parameter in the configuration of the simulator carries its source, and the values that remain assumptions are marked there. A few constants inside the code of the link models carry no source and count as assumptions. The sensitivity runs vary the main assumptions.

Paired simulation first, then the real prototype over emulated links

The radio links are emulated. No result on this page comes from a real radio network.

  1. Step 1

    Simulator

    A packet-level simulator written for this project replays six mobility scenarios on a 10 ms link grid. Six traffic classes run over UDP or TCP. TCP retransmits after a timeout of at least 200 ms and delivers in order, so a loss also delays the packets behind it. The forecaster never reads the true link state.

  2. Step 2

    Comparison

    Eight mechanisms meet exactly the same losses and delays in each run, over 30 random seeds per scenario. We report differences as paired means with 95% intervals, and we compare the budgeted families at equal budgets.

  3. Step 3

    Prototype

    The prototype is real software: a client and a server with an encrypted packet format, real timers, and the same policy module as the simulator. A relay between them replays the simulator's link traces. It ran on 8 October 2026, on the link models of that time, and was not repeated after the models were set from published measurements.

What we measure

Quality of service (QoS)
What the network delivers for each packet: its delay, the jitter, the time between arrivals, and loss or lateness.
Quality of experience (QoE)
What the user feels: the E-model score of a call, frozen frames of a video call, stalls of a streaming player (the rebuffering that YouTube and Netflix players count), and pauses of a download.
Lost or late
A packet that arrives after its deadline counts as lost, because the receiver can no longer use it. A voice packet has 80 ms from sending.
Jitter (RFC 3550)
The smoothed variation of transit time between consecutive packets, as an RTP receiver computes it.
Inter-packet arrival time
The time between two consecutive packets at the receiver: jitter as the receiver sees it. Voice packets leave every 20 ms, so a gap longer than 60 ms is a break in the call. We report its 99.9th percentile and the gaps longer than 60 ms per minute.

Masarain closes 83% of the gap to full redundancy with 19% of its extra data

The main comparison carries a call of 50 voice packets per second beside a control stream of 10 messages per second. We measure the gap from multipath scheduling by lowest round-trip time (RTT), which sends no copies, to full redundancy, which sends every packet on both networks whenever both are usable. All figures are means over six scenarios and 30 seeds. Extra data counts second copies and parity packets only. Keepalive probes on the idle path add a further 5.5 points for Masarain, 6.6 for lowest-RTT multipath, and 1.2 for full redundancy; with probes counted, Masarain sends 25% of the extra data that full redundancy sends.

Transport matters. Over TCP, a lost voice packet waits for a retransmission after at least 200 ms and holds back every packet behind it, so with one path 12.07% of the voice over TCP is lost or late, against 5.48% over UDP. With Masarain, a copy over the second network can arrive before the timeout fires, and the loss falls to 3.23%.

Buffered traffic needs no copy. A streaming player that holds 30 s of video rides out every outage in these runs, under every mechanism. Masarain sends no copies for it, against 81% extra data for full redundancy. Bulk data is not copied either, but moving it to the safer network cuts its pauses from 4.5 s per run with one path to 0.8 s.

Limits of this work

  • The link models follow published measurements, but they remain models: no recorded trace is replayed yet, and Wi-Fi contention and queueing under load are not modeled. Most remaining assumptions are marked in the code, and the sensitivity runs vary the main ones.
  • Each traffic class is tested alone, so the simulator does not yet show how classes that share one link affect each other.
  • Most of the gain comes from the budgeted ladder. A fixed signal threshold inside the same ladder already reaches 0.96% loss, and the forecast improves this to 0.83%.
  • Sudden failures cannot be forecast. The forecaster flags 0% of pulled cables in advance. The evaluation counts 12% of satellite obstructions as flagged, but that figure is chance coincidence with a scheduled reconfiguration, not a forecast: random timing alone gives about 12%. For both failures Masarain behaves like reactive duplication.
  • Early warning has a price: 55% of Wi-Fi alarms and 44% of 5G alarms are not followed by real degradation. The budget keeps those false alarms affordable.
  • No satellite terminal was available, so the satellite results come from simulation only.
  • The prototype forecasts Wi-Fi only, because a tethered laptop cannot read the signal of the 5G modem. It has run over emulated links only, and only on the link models of 8 October 2026. A field test on real Wi-Fi and 5G is the next step.
