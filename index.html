  1. 4 November 2026one walk

    One real Wi-Fi drop, measured

    A Linux VM bridged to the laptop's Wi-Fi pinged the phone hotspot while we carried the laptop away. Windows logged its own signal reading. The orange trigger sits on the last reply.

    This is single-path ping plus a replay through our simulator with a modeled 5G backup. It is not a live two-path call.

  2. zoom19 to 25 s, key Z

    The drop, magnified

    Switch after failure keeps sending into the dead Wi-Fi until its modeled 1.0 s detector fires, then spends a modeled 0.6 s reconnecting: 1,614 ms with no voice, 1,614 ms in both readings of the trace.

    Masarain had already moved the call to path B at 20.5 s: its per-path acknowledgment timeout declared the Wi-Fi down after max(4 × SRTT, 100 ms) without an acknowledgment. Through the drop its longest gap is 20 ms; the 24 ms for the whole replay comes later, at 34.5 s, on the modeled 5G.

    Replay of the measured trace, published reading, 10 seeds. Its unanswered probes sit on the fitted 21.69 ms grid, up to 0.1 s ahead of ping's clock here, so the hole opens at 21.23 s. Hatched: carried on the modeled 5G, never measured.

  3. t = 5.632 sfirst probe

    The VM starts probing the hotspot gateway

    A Linux VM, bridged to the laptop's Wi-Fi card, sent an echo request of 160 bytes to the gateway at 172.20.10.1. The protocol asked for one every 20 ms; the measured median while the link answered is 21.36 ms. Windows logged its own signal reading at the same time. The round trip sits at a baseline of 3.2 ms.

    At start-up the replay copies about 44 voice packets per run from 6.63 s, and in 6 of 10 seeds the per-path acknowledgment timeout moves the call to path B until 9.9 to 10.4 s, while the forecast risk is 0. Our reading of the replay: the first replies came back in batches, which tripped that timeout, and the return rule (risk below 0.075 held for 3 s) then kept the call on path B.

  4. t = 17.632 swindow start

    The round trip rises before any probe is lost

    In the one-second window from 17.6 s the median round trip is 19.0 ms, about 6 times the baseline, 2.4 s before the first lost probe. The signal reading has not moved: Windows still reports −30 dBm, as it has since the log began.

  5. t = 20.008 sfirst lost probe

    First lost probe, then one step in the signal reading

    Probe 655 never gets a reply. At 20.746 s Windows changes its signal reading for the first time, straight from −30 to −82 dBm, after holding one value for 20.7 s. A forecast that reads the signal trend had nothing to read before this step.

  6. t = 21.309 strigger

    The last reply, then silence

    Probe 710 returns after 41.1 ms. It is the last reply of the test. Ping keeps sending until 51.105 s and hears nothing back, so there was no reply for at least 29.8 s; the end of the outage was never observed.

    Near the end, ping printed “no answer yet” for sequence 1810 100 times without moving on (its next sends were refused inside the VM), and the VM reported the gateway unreachable from its own address.

  7. t = 73.582 sWindows clock

    Windows still reported connected 52.3 s after the last reply (derived)

    All 217 signal samples logged during the silence read connected at −82 dBm. Windows reports the Wi-Fi as disconnected 52.3 s after the last reply (derived), and as connected again at 144.3 s. The link-layer association state lagged behind reachability, so a session layer has to detect a dead path from its own packets.

    Derived across two clocks: the logs share no event, so their offset is assumed to be 0 s. Hatched stretches in the connected bar are logger stalls with no sample, up to 9.3 s, all after the last reply.

  8. replay10 seeds, 5G modeled

    The measured link, replayed through every mechanism

    Switch after failure uses one path at a time: a modeled 1.0 s detection plus 0.6 s reconnection set its 1,614 ms hole. Masarain's longest gap is 24 ms. Its call moves to path B at 20.5 s, when the per-path acknowledgment timeout declares the Wi-Fi down (no acknowledgment for max(4 × SRTT, 100 ms)); hatched means the modeled 5G.

    1.0 s is the simulator's modeled OS detection time (Linux mac80211 beacon loss is 0.72 s). It is generous next to the 52.3 s Windows took on 4 November (derived), not next to an acknowledgment-based detector: lowest-RTT multipath with the same ACK detector leaves 148 ms (published reading).

    Lost or late, published reading: 5.35% against 0.37% for 3.8% extra data.

  9. t = 20.83 sreplay, seed 0

    On this trace the forecast gave no lead time

    During the drop, copies resume in the 100 ms replay window 19.93 to 20.03 s that holds the first lost probe (20.008 s), from the rung that reacts to observed loss. At 20.5 s the per-path acknowledgment timeout declares the Wi-Fi down and the call moves to path B. The forecast first reaches 0.05 at 20.83 s, the fixed signal threshold fires at 21.23 s, and the last reply comes at 21.309 s.

    So neither the forecast nor rung 3 (risk ≥ 0.15 held for 1.0 s) moved the call. Signal-threshold duplication ties Masarain: 0.37% against 0.37%. The round trip rose 2.4 s before the first loss while the signal reading was frozen, but today the Wi-Fi forecast reads only the signal trend; adding the round-trip trend is an untested next step.

  10. scopeof this test

    What this capture does not show

    One walk, one hotspot, one laptop: single-path ping plus replay, not a live two-path call. Only the Wi-Fi was real; the 5G is the simulator's model, and Masarain did not run during the walk.

    The next test runs the prototype on real Wi-Fi and a real phone link at the same time.

What this test proves

  • A real 802.11 link, measured through a real Linux network stack, every probe stamped by the VM.
  • The link degraded before it failed: the median round trip rose from 3.2 to 19.0 ms, and 18 probes went unanswered before the last reply.
  • Windows lagged the link: all 217 samples logged during the silence read connected at −82 dBm, and the disconnect came 52.3 s after the last reply (derived).
  • Replayed through the published simulator, switch after failure leaves a 1,614 ms hole in the voice; Masarain's longest gap is 24 ms (published reading).

What it does not prove

  • Not a live two-path call: Masarain did not run in the VM, and only the Wi-Fi was real.
  • Not a 5G measurement: the backup in the replay is the simulator's model.
  • Not that the forecast beats a fixed signal threshold: on this trace they tie.
  • Not an outage length: no reply for at least 29.8 s, and the end was not observed.
  • Not a statistic: one walk, one phone hotspot, one laptop.

Loss against extra data

First the measured trace, replayed. Then the simulation over 6 scenarios and 30 paired seeds with link models built from published measurements: switch after failure loses 5.5% of voice packets, Masarain 0.83%. On the backup path Masarain sends 15.5% in copies plus 5.5% in probes; full redundancy reaches 0.46% for 81.4% plus 1.2%.

How Masarain decides

A session layer on the device estimates the risk that the active path loses packets within the next 1.5 s. For every packet with a deadline, it climbs a ladder only as far as that risk requires.

  1. 1

    Normal

    risk below 5%

    One copy of each packet travels on the preferred path. Probes keep the idle path's round trip measured.

  2. 2

    Risk ahead

    risk ≥ 5%, or a loss just seen

    Each packet with a deadline also travels on path B. One copy costs one token; the bucket refills at 20% of deadline traffic, so false alarms stay affordable.

  3. 3

    Risk sustained

    risk ≥ 15% for 1 s

    The flow moves to path B. It returns once the preferred path's risk has stayed below 7.5% for 3 s.

One voice packet on the wire

A voice frame of 160 bytes leaves every 20 ms. With the header and the tag it is 228 bytes on the wire. The header layout is read from the prototype's source.

flags, bit 0: this packet is a duplicate copy. The server tells a first copy from a duplicate with a replay window and acknowledges every copy once, back over the path it came from. The flag travels in clear, so an observer can see when the device starts copying, which hints that it expects a path switch.

Encryption
Between client and server at the session layer, with ChaCha20-Poly1305 and a static pre-shared key. The nonce is a BLAKE2s hash of the whole 24-byte header (session, sequence, sender timestamp, boot ID, path, and copy flag), so the two copies never share a nonce; the header never repeats under one key, so a repeat would need a 96-bit hash collision. Not externally reviewed.
Authenticated, not hidden
The header is associated data: nobody can alter it in transit, but an observer can read the type, path, copy flag, sequence number, and send time. The HMAC fallback sends the payload in clear.
Per-path acknowledgments
Each path keeps its own round-trip and loss estimate from the acknowledgments it receives. A path with no acknowledgment for max(4 × SRTT, 100 ms) is declared down and the flow leaves it at once, without waiting for the operating system.
Not built yet
No key exchange, no rekeying, no forward secrecy: the key is a static file shared in advance. The prototype carries voice in one direction.

Where each multipath mechanism works

Multipath TCPTransport layer, in the kernel: one TCP connection spread over several paths.
Multipath QUICTransport layer in user space, over UDP; when to send a copy is left open.
3GPP ATSSSIn the 5G device and the core network: the operator steers or splits traffic between 5G and Wi-Fi.
SD-WANA network-layer overlay on an edge router that steers or duplicates traffic by policy.
MasarainA session-layer overlay over UDP, on the device or on a gateway; no operator is needed.

Limits of this work

  • Most of the gain comes from the budgeted ladder.A fixed signal threshold in the same ladder already reaches 0.96% of voice lost or late; the forecast takes it to 0.83%.
  • Sudden failures give no warning.The forecaster flags 0% of pulled cables in advance. The 12% of satellite obstructions it flags equals chance overlap with a scheduled reconfiguration.
  • Early warning has a price.55% of Wi-Fi alarms and 44% of 5G alarms are not followed by real degradation. The budget keeps those copies bounded.
  • The prototype ran over emulated links only.Its recorded runs date from 8 October 2026, on the link models of that day. It forecasts Wi-Fi only, because a tethered laptop cannot read the 5G modem's signal.
  • The real test is one walk.One hotspot, one laptop, single-path ping plus replay. The 5G backup in the replay is a model, and no satellite terminal was available.
  • Models are models.The link models follow published measurements, but the simulator does not model Wi-Fi contention or queueing under load, and each traffic class is tested alone.
